## You are in a very large club

Nearly every sexually active person gets HPV at some point. Around one in six adults has genital herpes, and most never know. A positive result says you had sex, nothing more. It says nothing about who you are.

## What changes and what does not

You keep dating, keep having sex, keep being wanted. What changes is small and practical: a conversation you learn to have, and for herpes, maybe a prescription.

## Herpes: outbreaks and medicine

Outbreaks usually get milder and rarer with time. Daily medicine can cut how often they come and how easily herpes passes on. During an outbreak, when sores or tingling are present, skipping sex for those days is the caring move; the higher-risk window passes, and so does the wait.

## HPV: it usually clears

Most HPV clears on its own within a couple of years. There is no treatment for the virus itself, just for what it can cause: warts are treatable, and [screening](/vaccines) catches cell changes early. The vaccine still helps you, even after one type has shown up.

## Telling partners

Shorter and easier than you fear, and it gets easier every time. [How to tell a partner](/tell-a-partner) has words you can borrow.

## The feelings part

The stigma is the worst symptom, and it fades. Clinics have this conversation every day, and so do millions of people with full dating lives. Give it a few weeks before you decide what it means.
