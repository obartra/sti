## Thirty seconds now beats weeks of wondering

One question before sex saves a lot of guessing after. "Are we using condoms?" has never ruined a night that was going to happen; most people are relieved someone else asked first.

## Asking about protection

Say what you use and ask what they use. "Condoms work for you?" "I'm on PrEP. You on anything?" If you have a boundary, name it before clothes come off; it is much easier standing up. [What each tool covers](/protection) helps you know what you are asking for.

## Asking about status

"When were you last tested?" is a normal question, and offering yours first makes it easy: "I got tested last month, all clear. You?" If neither of you knows, that is not a dealbreaker, it is a plan: [testing is quick](/testing) and you can go together.

## Consent is a check-in, not a form

Ask before you change what you are doing, and notice the answer. "This good?" covers most of it. A yes earlier is not a yes forever; anyone can change their mind mid-way, including you.

## Saying no, hearing no

"Not tonight" and "not without a condom" are complete sentences; you do not owe a reason. When you get a no, take it well and move on. How someone hears a no tells you plenty.

## When the plan changes

No condom around, or the one you had broke? There are other ends to a night, and there is next time. If something already happened that worries you, PEP can still stop HIV within 72 hours, and a clinic can sort the rest.
